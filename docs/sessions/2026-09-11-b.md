## The problem

The run ends, the display switches to counting up, and overrun time is never
logged (by design, §6a). If Santi isn't looking, the minutes between the real
end and the moment he notices are simply lost.

## What got built — 6f. End-of-session alert

Three signals fire together from `completeAtZero()`, chosen because they fail
in different ways:

| Signal | Works when | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| WebAudio chime | tab buried, window behind something | volume down |
| OS notification | volume down, another app focused | no permission, or `file://` |
| Tab-title flash | no permission, no audio | he isn't looking at the tab bar |

All three are **one-shot** (his call): no repeating chime, no blinking title,
nothing to dismiss.

- **Chime is synthesised, not an audio file.** Two sine tones a fifth apart
  (A5 + E6, second one 160ms late so they ring together), each with a 20ms
  attack / exponential decay envelope — a bare oscillator switched on and off
  steps the waveform in one sample and that discontinuity is an audible click.
  An `.mp3` would have been the first asset in a project that is deliberately
  one file with no build step, and would 404 differently under `file://` than
  under Pages.
- **AudioContext is primed on the first click or keypress anywhere on the
  page**, not on Start. Browsers refuse a context that wasn't opened in a user
  gesture, and the chime fires 25 minutes after any gesture — so the context is
  opened early and left running. Hooking it to *any* interaction (rather than
  Start) is what keeps a mid-session page refresh from ending silently.
- **Notification permission is requested on the first Start click**, once.
  Not on load: gesture-less prompts are increasingly ignored by browsers and
  reflexively dismissed by people.
- **Notification carries `tag: "focus-timer-end"`**, so a second one replaces
  the first instead of stacking a column of them over a long day. Body names
  the category when one is set.
- **Title flash restores itself** on a 20s timeout or the moment the window
  regains focus, whichever is first — coming back to the page is the dismissal.

### Waking up on time (the part that isn't obvious)

Chrome throttles timers hard in a hidden tab — exactly the case the alert
exists for. A new `scheduleAlarm()` sets a single long `setTimeout` aimed at
the zero crossing, which is not throttled the same way.

It only **nudges**: it calls `updateDisplay()` (idempotent) and lets
`completeAtZero()` end the run as it always has. Firing the alert directly from
the timeout would create a second path that ends a session, and the
double-logging guard exists precisely because those are expensive to get right.

`clearAlarm()` is called from `pause()`, `reset()`, `setDurationMinutes()` and
`completeAtZero()`; `scheduleAlarm()` from `start()` and once at the end of
`initApp` so a mid-session refresh re-arms.

## Decisions made

- **One chime, then quiet** (his call) over repeat-until-acknowledged.
- **Tab title flashes once and reverts**, not alternating forever (his call).
- **`completeAtZero()` is the only caller of `announceSessionEnd()`** — "exactly
  one alert per session" is then true by construction, not by a flag.
- **Every alert path is wrapped in try/catch and degrades to silence.** Audio
  and notifications are a nicety; neither may ever break the timer.

## Known limit — flagged

**OS notifications do not work over `file://`.** Browsers treat it as an opaque
origin and refuse the permission outright. The chime and the title flash work
everywhere. This resolves itself the moment the page moves to GitHub Pages —
it's now a real reason to do that, where before it was only convenience.
